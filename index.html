SECTION 01

Client Types in Healthcare Staffing

In US Healthcare Staffing, the "client" is the hospital, health system, or facility that needs staff. Understanding how they buy staffing services defines your entire recruitment approach.

MSP

Managed Service Provider

THE GATEKEEPER MODEL

An MSP is a third-party company that manages all staffing vendors on behalf of a healthcare facility. The MSP sits between the hospital and staffing agencies — all orders, submissions, and placements go through them.

Your agency is a sub-vendor to the MSP. You do not communicate directly with the hospital's hiring managers.

High Volume Competitive Margins VMS Driven Speed Critical
REAL WORLD EXAMPLE
HCA Healthcare hires Aya Healthcare as its MSP. Aya opens orders to agencies like HonorVet, who submit candidates through Aya's portal — not directly to HCA.
DIRECT

Direct Client

THE PARTNERSHIP MODEL

A Direct Client is a hospital or healthcare facility that contracts directly with your agency — no MSP in the middle. You negotiate rates, receive orders, and submit candidates straight to the facility's HR or hiring manager.

Higher margins, stronger relationships, and more control over the process. Building direct relationships is a strategic priority.

Higher Margins Direct Communication Relationship-Based Account Development
REAL WORLD EXAMPLE
A 200-bed community hospital in Texas signs a staffing contract directly with HonorVet. The recruiter works directly with the CNO (Chief Nursing Officer) on all open positions.
VMS

Vendor Management System

THE TECHNOLOGY PLATFORM

A VMS is a software platform (not a company) that facilitates the ordering, submission, tracking, and billing of contingent workforce. MSPs typically use a VMS to manage vendor activity digitally.

Think of VMS as the operating system and MSP as the company running it. Common VMS platforms: Fieldglass, Beeline, IQNavigator, Shiftwise.

Automated Workflow Digital Submissions Compliance Tracking Integrated with MSP
REAL WORLD EXAMPLE
The MSP for Kaiser Permanente uses SAP Fieldglass as its VMS. Every time HonorVet submits a Travel RN, it's done through Fieldglass — with automated status updates, rate validation, and credentialing timelines.

SECTION 02

Employment Types in Healthcare Staffing

Every healthcare professional you recruit falls into one of three employment categories. Understanding these is non-negotiable — it affects pay, duration, benefits, and your pitch.

✈️
Travel
TEMPORARY · ASSIGNMENT-BASED · PREMIUM PAY

Travel nurses and allied professionals accept temporary assignments (typically 13 weeks) at facilities outside their permanent tax home — usually 50+ miles away. They receive a tax-free stipend (housing, meals, incidentals) in addition to base pay.

This is the core product of most healthcare staffing firms. High compensation attracts clinicians; facilities get flexibility to cover short-term staffing needs.

Per Diem
FLEXIBLE · SHIFT-BASED · LOCAL

Per Diem (Latin for "per day") clinicians work on an as-needed basis, picking up individual shifts at local facilities — often with 24–48 hours notice. They typically live locally and have no tax-home advantage, so no stipends.

Ideal for professionals who want flexibility over stability. Facilities use Per Diem to fill last-minute callouts and census fluctuations without long-term commitments.

🏥
Permanent (Perm)
DIRECT HIRE · FULL-TIME · LONG-TERM

Permanent placement means the healthcare professional becomes a direct employee of the facility — not an employee of your agency. You earn a one-time placement fee (usually 15–25% of first-year salary) from the client.

Perm staffing is relationship-driven. The clinician is done with your agency post-placement, but strong Perm work builds deep client trust and positions you for ongoing business.

SECTION 03

Contract Lingo You Must Know

Healthcare staffing has its own language. These are the terms you'll hear every day — on calls, in VMS portals, and in compliance conversations. Know them cold.

These terms appear in job orders, contracts, VMS portals, and client calls. Understanding them will help you work faster, communicate professionally, and avoid costly errors.
● CORE CONTRACT TERMS
CONTRACT
The formal agreement between the staffing agency and the healthcare facility (or between the agency and the clinician). Outlines assignment length, rates, duties, cancellation policy, and compliance requirements.
ASSIGNMENT
The specific travel or per diem engagement. An assignment has a start date, end date, location, shift type, and unit. A clinician may complete multiple consecutive assignments at the same or different facilities.
EXTENSION
When a facility requests a clinician to continue beyond the original contract end date — often in increments of 4 or 13 weeks. Extensions are high-value because they require no new sourcing cost.
GUARANTEED HOURS
The minimum number of hours per week the agency guarantees to pay the clinician, even if the facility cancels shifts. Typically 36 hours for a 3×12 schedule. Critical in negotiating contracts.
CANCELLATION POLICY
The notice period required (by either party) to end a contract early without penalty. Standard is 2–4 weeks' notice. Some contracts allow "shift cancellation" — cancelling a shift within 24–48 hours (usually without pay).
FLOAT
When a clinician is reassigned to a different unit or floor within the facility based on patient census needs. Travel contracts often include float clauses — the nurse must be willing to float within their competency.
ON-CALL / CALL-BACK
A scheduling requirement where the clinician must be reachable and ready to report to work on short notice. On-call hours and call-back hours are typically compensated at a different rate.
● CREDENTIALING & COMPLIANCE TERMS
CREDENTIALS
All required documentation a clinician must provide before starting an assignment: licenses, certifications (BLS, ACLS, PALS), physical exam, drug screen, background check, immunization records, references, and skills checklists.
SKILL CHECKLIST
A self-assessment form where the clinician rates their proficiency in specific clinical skills relevant to their specialty. Facilities use this to verify competency before approving a submission.
COMPACT LICENSE
A multi-state nursing license under the Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC) that allows RNs to practice in multiple member states without obtaining individual state licenses. Essential for travel nurses.
ORIENTATION
The mandatory onboarding period at a facility before a clinician begins patient care. Typically 1–3 days. Usually paid at a standard rate (not premium). Travel contracts specify orientation terms upfront.
● STAFFING OPERATIONS TERMS
SUBMISSION
The act of presenting a candidate's profile to the facility (via MSP, VMS, or direct email). A submission includes: resume, credentials, references, rates, and availability. Speed of submission is critical in competitive markets.
OFFER / PACKAGE
The compensation package presented to the clinician: base hourly rate + tax-free stipends + overtime terms + benefits. Recruiters must build packages that are competitive but still profitable for the agency.
DO NOT RETURN (DNR)
A flag placed on a clinician by a facility — they are not allowed to return to that facility for any future assignment. Can result from performance issues, conduct concerns, or policy violations.
EXCLUSIVE / NON-EXCLUSIVE
An exclusive order can only be filled by one agency (your agency). A non-exclusive order is shared across multiple agencies. Non-exclusive orders require speed — submit your best candidate first.
BLOCK BOOKING
A facility pre-commits to a set number of contracted nurses over a specified time period, often at a slightly lower bill rate in exchange for guaranteed availability. Common with large health systems.
📘
Study Tip: When you see a term in a VMS order or client email that you don't recognize, never guess — flag it to your senior recruiter immediately. A misunderstood cancellation clause or float requirement can create costly issues after placement.
SECTION 04

Bill Rate vs Pay Rate

This is the financial engine of healthcare staffing. Understanding the spread between what you charge the client and what you pay the clinician is fundamental to everything you do.

💰

Bill Rate

The hourly rate your agency charges the client (facility) for the clinician's services. This is what the hospital pays. It includes all costs associated with employing and placing the clinician.

Charged to the CLIENT
💵

Pay Rate

The hourly rate your agency pays the clinician as their base taxable wage. This is the starting point of their total compensation package. The pay rate does NOT include stipends.

✦ GOLDEN RULES OF RATE MANAGEMENT
01

Never reveal the bill rate to the candidate

The bill rate is proprietary business information between your agency and the client. Candidates should only know their pay rate and package. Sharing bill rates creates negotiation friction and can breach client contracts.

COMPLIANCE RULE
02

Protect your margin — never negotiate below the floor

Every agency has a minimum margin threshold (often 15–20%). If a candidate asks for more pay and you're already at the floor, you must either renegotiate the bill rate with the client or decline to proceed. Never take a loss on a placement.

PROFITABILITY RULE
03

Blended rate vs straight hourly — know the difference

Some packages blend pay rate + stipend into a single "all-in" hourly figure for simplicity. Always know which model you're quoting so the candidate understands their taxable vs non-taxable income breakdown correctly.

TRANSPARENCY RULE
04

Overtime = Bill Rate × 1.5 (for the clinician's base only)

Overtime pay is calculated on the base pay rate, not the full package. Stipends are not subject to overtime multipliers. Be sure you and the client agree on the OT calculation method upfront to avoid billing disputes.

SECTION 05

Introduction to GSA.gov & Stipend Rates

The U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) sets the federal per diem rates used as the legal benchmark for tax-free travel stipends in healthcare staffing. Every recruiter must know how to use this tool.

🏛️

What is GSA.gov?

The U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) is a federal government agency that, among other functions, publishes official Per Diem rates for all U.S. locations. These rates define the maximum daily allowances for lodging and meals & incidental expenses (M&IE) for federal employees traveling on official business.

In healthcare staffing, these rates serve as the IRS-recognized benchmark for structuring tax-free stipend packages for travel clinicians.

Official Government Source
⚖️

Why GSA Rates Matter in Staffing

The IRS allows travel nurses to receive tax-free stipends (housing + M&IE) only when the amounts are within federally accepted per diem limits. GSA rates are the most widely used reference point.

Paying stipends significantly above GSA rates creates tax liability risk for the clinician and potential compliance exposure for your agency. Always anchor your stipend packages to GSA data.

IRS Compliance Anchor
✦ THE TWO COMPONENTS OF GSA PER DIEM
🏠

Lodging Rate

The maximum nightly hotel/lodging allowance for the location. Varies significantly by city — San Francisco will be much higher than rural Kansas. Published as a daily (per night) dollar amount.

HOW RECRUITERS USE IT
Multiply daily lodging rate × 30 days = max monthly housing stipend. Divide by ~4.33 weeks = weekly housing stipend ceiling.
🍽️

M&IE Rate

Meals & Incidental Expenses — a flat daily rate covering meals, tips, laundry, and minor personal expenses incurred while traveling. The M&IE rate is further broken down by breakfast, lunch, dinner, and incidentals.

HOW RECRUITERS USE IT
Multiply daily M&IE × 7 days = weekly M&IE stipend. Add weekly housing stipend to get total weekly non-taxable stipend package.
✦ STEP-BY-STEP: HOW TO LOOK UP STIPEND RATES BY ZIP CODE
01

Go to the GSA Per Diem Rates page

Open your browser and navigate to GSA.gov. In the top search bar, type "Per Diem Rates" — or go directly to: gsa.gov/travel/plan-book/per-diem-rates. This is a free, publicly accessible government tool — no login required.

FREE & PUBLIC TOOL
02

Select the Fiscal Year

GSA per diem rates are published by Fiscal Year (FY), which runs October 1 – September 30. Always select the current fiscal year for the most accurate rates. Rates typically update each October — if a contract spans two FYs, note that rates may change mid-assignment.

IMPORTANT: USE CURRENT FY
03

Enter the Destination ZIP Code or City

In the search field, enter either the ZIP code of the assignment location or the city name and state. Using ZIP code is more precise — cities can span multiple counties with different rates. Always search by the ZIP code given on the job order for accuracy.

USE ZIP CODE FOR PRECISION
04

Read the Results — Lodging + M&IE

The results page shows two numbers: the Lodging rate (per night) and the M&IE rate (per day), often broken down by month (some locations have seasonal rate variations). Note both numbers — you need both to build a complete stipend package.

NOTE BOTH NUMBERS

✦ RECRUITER RULES FOR GSA COMPLIANCE
✅

DO — Best Practices

Always look up GSA rates before building a package — not after.
Use the assignment ZIP code, not just the city name.
Check for seasonal rate changes if an assignment spans October 1st.
Keep a screenshot of the GSA rate you used in the candidate's file for compliance records.
When in doubt, stay at or below GSA — not above.

❌

DON'T — Common Mistakes

Never estimate or guess stipend amounts without checking GSA.
Don't use last year's rates — they may have changed.
Never pay a candidate who does not have a valid tax home as if they qualify for travel stipends.
Don't confuse M&IE with lodging — they are separate line items.
Never offer a stipend significantly above the GSA ceiling to win a candidate.

SECTION 06

Real-World Scenarios

Apply what you've learned. Read each scenario, think through your answer, then reveal the expert response.

SCENARIO 01 · CLIENT TYPES

You receive an order from "Aya Healthcare" for a Travel RN — ICU at St. Mary's Hospital in Phoenix. Who is your client and what type of relationship is this?

Think about: Who sent you the order? Who will the clinician work for? Who pays your agency?

Your client is Aya Healthcare (the MSP) — not St. Mary's Hospital. This is an MSP relationship. You submit the candidate through Aya's VMS portal, Aya interviews and approves the candidate, and Aya invoices the hospital. Your agency is a sub-vendor. Key implication: margins are lower than direct, and you must move fast — other agencies are submitting to the same order.
SCENARIO 02 · EMPLOYMENT TYPE

A nurse tells you she lives in Dallas and wants to pick up shifts at local hospitals during the weekends. She doesn't want to relocate. What employment type is best and can she receive housing stipends?

Per Diem is the right employment type for this nurse. She works locally, shift-by-shift, with no relocation required. She cannot receive tax-free housing or M&IE stipends because she is working near her permanent tax home — there's no duplicated living expense to reimburse. Her compensation will be a straight hourly rate, which is typically higher than a travel nurse's base pay rate to compensate for the lack of benefits.
SCENARIO 03 · BILL RATE VS PAY RATE

The bill rate for a Travel RN position is $85/hr. Your agency needs a minimum 18% margin. The clinician's stipends cost the agency the equivalent of $28/hr. What is the maximum pay rate you can offer?

Step-by-step:
• Bill Rate: $85/hr
• Required Margin (18%): $85 × 0.18 = $15.30/hr must stay with agency
• Available for candidate costs: $85 − $15.30 = $69.70/hr
• Stipend cost: $28/hr
• Maximum Pay Rate: $69.70 − $28 = $41.70/hr

Anything above $41.70 as pay rate drops you below your 18% margin floor. Present the candidate with $41/hr pay + $28/hr stipend equivalent = $69/hr total package.
SCENARIO 04 · CONTRACT LINGO

A Travel RN's contract says "36 guaranteed hours per week, with a float clause to PCU." Midway through the assignment, the hospital calls out the nurse for 2 shifts due to low census. What does this mean for the nurse's pay?

Because the contract includes 36 guaranteed hours, the nurse must be paid for those hours even when the facility cancels shifts due to low census. The staffing agency pays the nurse for the missed shifts per the guarantee, then seeks reimbursement from the facility per contract terms. The float clause means the hospital may reassign her to PCU (Progressive Care Unit) — she agreed to this upfront and cannot refuse without breaching contract. Always review float and guarantee terms with candidates before placement.
